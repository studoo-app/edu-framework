# Major migrations 

Major migrations are important milestones in the life of a project. 
They can bring significant changes to the code structure, features or dependencies.

It is essential to understand these changes in order to update your project safely.

## Why migrate?

Major migrations are necessary for several reasons:

- **Performance improvements**: New versions can bring significant performance improvements.
- **Bug fixes**: Updates usually fix bugs and security vulnerabilities.
- **New features**: New versions can introduce new features or improvements. (evolution)
- **Compatibility**: Older versions can become obsolete and no longer be supported.
- **Security**: Security updates are essential to protect your project against known vulnerabilities.
- **Maintainability**: Obsolete versions can become hard to maintain due to lack of support.
- **Compliance**: Updates may be needed to comply with current standards and regulations.
- **Innovation**: Updates can introduce innovative technologies that improve your project.
- **Training**: Major migrations are an opportunity to learn new practices and technologies.
- **Reputation**: Up-to-date projects are generally better perceived by users and contributors.

## How to migrate?

Major migrations can be complex and require careful planning. Here are some steps to follow for a successful migration:

- **Assess the changes**: Analyze the changes introduced by the new version to understand their impact on your project.
- **Compatibility testing**: Check your project's compatibility with the new version by running thorough tests.
- **Data backup**: Make sure you back up your data before migrating to avoid any loss.
- **Dependency updates**: Update your project's dependencies to ensure compatibility with the new version.
- **Gradual migration**: If possible, migrate gradually to limit risks and make debugging easier.
- **Communication**: Inform users and contributors about the migration to avoid confusion or disruption.
- **Training**: Make sure your team members are trained on the new features and best practices.
- **Monitoring and maintenance**: Keep a close eye on your project after the migration to detect any problems and fix them quickly.
- **Feedback**: Collect feedback
- **Documentation**: Update the documentation to reflect the changes introduced by the migration.
- **Celebration**: Celebrate the success of your migration and the benefits it brings to your project.
